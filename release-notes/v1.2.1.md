## What's changed

- **No more smeared picture in motion after a Wi-Fi hiccup.** When Wi-Fi lost a frame, the frames after it were decoded against an older picture. That damage was copied into every frame that followed and smeared across anything that moved. The PS5 only sends a fresh full picture when asked, and in this case nobody asked: in one 2-hour session the damage stayed for up to 12 minutes at a time.
  - Now, as soon as a frame is decoded against a missing picture, the app asks the PS5 for a fresh one. It usually arrives within a fraction of a second.
  - The picture doesn't freeze while it waits; it keeps playing and cleans up when the fresh picture arrives.
  - It asks at most every 1.5 seconds, so a very bad Wi-Fi moment doesn't flood it with requests.
  - The log says "Picture damaged by a lost frame, asking for a fresh one" each time.
- **Tested** by building and replaying the H.265 demo clip. The fix itself needs a real PS5 and a lost frame, so it hasn't been seen working yet.

Sound and connecting weren't changed.

## How to update

On v0.1.22 or later, you don't need to do anything. The app installs this version by itself, either right after it opens or once audio and video are both off.

Or download `PS-Stream-v1.2.1-windows-x64.zip`, extract it, and open `PS Stream.exe`. Keep the folder together. Windows may warn you because the app isn't signed: choose **More info**, then **Run anyway**. Your pairing and settings carry over.

If Windows Defender removes `PS Stream.exe`, open **Windows Security > Virus & threat protection > Protection history**, open the PS Stream entry and choose **Restore**.

## Known issues

- With the same PSN account you play on, the DualSense stops working on the PS5 while the app is connected. Pair the app with a second PSN account to keep using it.
- Turning video on or off during a session reconnects. The PS5 takes 6 to 8 seconds to accept the new connection.
- A gamepad on the PC hasn't been confirmed to control the PS5 yet. If it doesn't work for you, note the time and share your `log.txt`.
- Fast motion at low bitrates can still look blocky. That comes from the PS5's own compression; a higher quality preset helps.
