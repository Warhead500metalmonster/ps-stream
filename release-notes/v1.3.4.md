# PS Stream 1.3.4

## What's new

- On an NVIDIA RTX graphics card, the video is now enlarged with RTX Video Super Resolution instead of a plain stretch. The card's AI cores sharpen a 720p stream on a bigger screen and clean up the blur compression leaves. In a test with a 720p H.265 clip in a 1765-pixel-wide window, edges came out about 20% sharper. Discord shares the sharper picture too.
- It needs "RTX Video Enhancement > Super Resolution" turned on in the NVIDIA App (or NVIDIA Control Panel > Adjust video image settings). The log says whether the driver accepted it. Other graphics cards draw the picture as before.

Nothing about connecting or the sound changed.

## How to update

On v0.1.22 or later, you don't need to do anything. The app installs this version by itself, either right after it opens or once audio and video are both off.

Or download `PS-Stream-v1.3.4-windows-x64.zip`, extract it, and open `PS Stream.exe`. Keep the folder together. Windows may warn you because the app isn't signed: choose **More info**, then **Run anyway**. Your pairing and settings carry over.

If Windows Defender removes `PS Stream.exe`, open **Windows Security > Virus & threat protection > Protection history**, open the PS Stream entry and choose **Restore**.

## Known issues

- With the same PSN account you play on, the DualSense stops working on the PS5 while the app is connected with a gamepad. Pair the app with a second PSN account to keep using it.
- Turning video on or off during a session reconnects. The PS5 takes 6 to 8 seconds to accept the new connection.
- Motion controls and the adaptive triggers of a real DualSense aren't sent from a PC pad.
