## What's changed

- **Less silence after a Wi-Fi stall.** When weak Wi-Fi holds sound back for a moment, everything it held arrives at once. If that was more than **Max delay**, the app threw all of it away and waited for new sound, so every stall was followed by a second silence. Now the sound that's already queued keeps playing, and only the extra is left out, so the sound is back at its usual delay without a pause. In a simulation of a real evening on bad Wi-Fi (stalls of 0.4 to 0.9 s), silence went from 5.1 s to 3.9 s a minute. The rest is the stall itself, which no app can fill. On good Wi-Fi nothing changes.
- The log says when this happens ("Audio backlog of … ms after a burst"), and the connection report counts it under "sound caught up".

Connecting wasn't changed.

## How to update

On v0.1.22 or later, you don't need to do anything. The app installs this version by itself, either right after it opens or once audio and video are both off. On older versions, reopen the app and press **Update** at the bottom.

Or download `PS-Stream-v0.1.43-windows-x64.zip`, extract it, and open `PS Stream.exe`. Keep the folder together. Windows may warn you because the app isn't signed: choose **More info**, then **Run anyway**. Your pairing and settings carry over.

If Windows Defender removes `PS Stream.exe` after an update, open **Windows Security > Virus & threat protection > Protection history**, open the PS Stream entry and choose **Restore**. See [Windows Defender says it's a trojan](https://github.com/Warhead500metalmonster/ps-stream#windows-defender-says-its-a-trojan).

## Known issues

- With the same PSN account you play on, the DualSense stops working on the PS5 while the app is connected. Pair the app with a second PSN account to keep using it.
- Turning video on or off during a session reconnects. The PS5 takes about 8 seconds to accept the new connection.
- Some people report that a gamepad on the PC doesn't control the PS5. If that happens to you, note the time and share your `log.txt`.
- The app isn't signed, so Windows Defender may still flag a new version now and then.
