# PS Stream 1.3.7

## What's new

- Fixes the picture that starts sharp and smooth and turns bad after 20 to 40 minutes, then stays bad. When the PS5 is short on bitrate, its encoder leaves frames out on purpose. The app took every left-out frame for one lost on Wi-Fi, told the PS5 the picture was corrupt and asked for a whole new picture. The PS5 then lowered the bitrate further and sent big pictures, which made it leave out even more frames. In two sessions on 2026-11-21 the bitrate fell from 3 to 0.8 Mbps after about 30 minutes and stayed there for up to two hours, although the Wi-Fi to the modem was fine.
- The app now tells the two apart by the packet numbers, which run on without a gap when frames were left out. Left-out frames are no longer reported or held back. Frames really lost on Wi-Fi are handled as before.
- The log says when the PS5 left frames out.

Nothing about connecting or the sound changed.

## How to update

On v0.1.22 or later, you don't need to do anything. The app installs this version by itself, either right after it opens or once audio and video are both off.

Or download `PS-Stream-v1.3.7-windows-x64.zip`, extract it, and open `PS Stream.exe`. Keep the folder together. Windows may warn you because the app isn't signed: choose **More info**, then **Run anyway**. Your pairing and settings carry over.

If Windows Defender removes `PS Stream.exe`, open **Windows Security > Virus & threat protection > Protection history**, open the PS Stream entry and choose **Restore**.

## Known issues

- With the same PSN account you play on, the DualSense stops working on the PS5 while the app is connected with a gamepad. Pair the app with a second PSN account to keep using it.
- Turning video on or off during a session reconnects. The PS5 takes 6 to 8 seconds to accept the new connection.
- Motion controls and the adaptive triggers of a real DualSense aren't sent from a PC pad.
