## What's changed

- **Less likely to be taken for a trojan by Windows Defender.** On one PC, Microsoft Defender removed v0.1.41 right after it updated, calling it `Trojan:Win32/Bearfoos.A!ml`. The `!ml` means a machine-learning guess, not known malware. PS Stream isn't signed, and until now its files also carried no details at all about what they are. Both `PS Stream.exe` files now say under **Properties > Details** that they're PS Stream, which version, who makes it and its license, the way other programs do. Unsigned programs without these details are flagged more often.
- **The README says what to do if it happens anyway:** how to get the file back from Protection history, and how to report it to Microsoft as safe.

Nothing else changed. Everything from v0.1.41 is here, including the larger audio buffer for bursty Wi-Fi.

## How to update

On v0.1.22 or later, you don't need to do anything. The app installs this version by itself, either right after it opens or once audio and video are both off. On older versions, reopen the app and press **Update** at the bottom.

Or download `PS-Stream-v0.1.42-windows-x64.zip`, extract it, and open `PS Stream.exe`. Keep the folder together. Windows may warn you because the app isn't signed: choose **More info**, then **Run anyway**. Your pairing and settings carry over.

If Windows Defender removes `PS Stream.exe` after an update, open **Windows Security > Virus & threat protection > Protection history**, open the PS Stream entry and choose **Restore**. See [Windows Defender says it's a trojan](https://github.com/Warhead500metalmonster/ps-stream#windows-defender-says-its-a-trojan).

## Known issues

- With the same PSN account you play on, the DualSense stops working on the PS5 while the app is connected. Pair the app with a second PSN account to keep using it.
- Turning video on or off during a session reconnects. The PS5 takes about 8 seconds to accept the new connection.
- Some people report that a gamepad on the PC doesn't control the PS5. If that happens to you, note the time and share your `log.txt`.
- The app isn't signed, so Windows Defender may still flag a new version now and then.
