## The GTA 6 release

Everything since v0.1.38, tested together, for playing on weak Wi-Fi.

### Sound on weak Wi-Fi
- **The audio buffer grows to the gaps your Wi-Fi actually has.** Real sessions had sound arrive in pieces, with gaps from 0.15 s up to 1.4 s and often no packet loss at all. The buffer used to stop at 0.1 s, so every longer gap was a dropout. Now, after a dropout, it grows straight to the gap that caused it:
  - up to **0.5 s in audio only**, where the picture is on the TV and only the sound comes to the PC,
  - up to **0.3 s with video**, so the sound stays close to the picture.

  On Wi-Fi that delivers sound evenly, it stays small and shrinks again after a quiet minute.
- **No second silence after a stall.** When Wi-Fi releases held-back sound all at once, the app keeps playing and skips only the extra, instead of throwing everything away and waiting.
- **Fewer dropouts in the first seconds of a stream**, when the PS5 sends sound in bursts.
- **Max delay works.** It sets the limit now, and its default is 400 ms. Set it lower if you'd rather have the sound closer to the picture than fewer dropouts.

In simulations built from real sessions on this kind of Wi-Fi, silence from dropouts in audio only went from about 5 s to under 2 s a minute. When the Wi-Fi brings nothing at all for more than half a second, no app can fill that gap.

### Everything else
- **A maximized video window goes full screen.** `F11` or `Esc` brings back the window, at the same size and place as before.
- **Updates install by themselves again after a bad one.** A version that fails to start is skipped only by itself, not every version after it.
- **Releases are built on GitHub** from the code in the repository. Before a release goes out, the build checks that every library in it loads and that the app's sound, video and gamepad parts start.
- **Less likely to be taken for a trojan by Windows Defender.** Both exes now carry their name, version, maker and license. The README explains what to do if Defender flags it anyway.
- **The download includes third-party licenses** in `THIRD-PARTY-NOTICES.txt`.
- **The log shows where gamepad presses go**, to find out why a gamepad doesn't work for some people.

Connecting wasn't changed.

## How to update

On v0.1.22 or later, you don't need to do anything. The app installs this version by itself, either right after it opens or once audio and video are both off. On older versions, reopen the app and press **Update** at the bottom.

Or download `PS-Stream-v0.1.44-windows-x64.zip`, extract it, and open `PS Stream.exe`. Keep the folder together. Windows may warn you because the app isn't signed: choose **More info**, then **Run anyway**. Your pairing and settings carry over.

If Windows Defender removes `PS Stream.exe` after an update, open **Windows Security > Virus & threat protection > Protection history**, open the PS Stream entry and choose **Restore**. See [Windows Defender says it's a trojan](https://github.com/Warhead500metalmonster/ps-stream#windows-defender-says-its-a-trojan).

## Known issues

- With the same PSN account you play on, the DualSense stops working on the PS5 while the app is connected. Pair the app with a second PSN account to keep using it.
- Turning video on or off during a session reconnects. The PS5 takes about 8 seconds to accept the new connection.
- Some people report that a gamepad on the PC doesn't control the PS5. If that happens to you, note the time and share your `log.txt`.
- The app isn't signed, so Windows Defender may still flag a new version now and then.
